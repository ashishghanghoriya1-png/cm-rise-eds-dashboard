### Comprehensive Qualitative Research Report

#### 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & KEY HIGHLIGHTS

The Continuous Professional Development Ecosystem Diagnostic Study (CPD EDS) conducted in Gwalior district revealed critical insights into the professional development landscape for teachers. This study focused on a Social Science teacher with 24 years of experience in a single-teacher school setting. Key highlights include the substantial administrative burden, the teacher’s preference for in-person training over digital platforms, and the limited applicability of peer learning communities in the context of a single-teacher school. The analysis emphasizes the need for tailored professional development interventions that address the specific constraints and challenges faced by single-teacher schools in Gwalior.

#### 2. RESPONDENT PROFILE & CONTEXTUAL CONSTRAINTS

**Staffing Context:** The teacher works in a single-teacher school with a total of around 103 students. The staffing situation is challenging as the sole teacher is responsible for all academic and administrative tasks, creating a heavy workload.

**Non-Academic Burden:** The teacher's responsibilities extend far beyond classroom teaching, including managing student documentation, attending to administrative tasks such as APAR ID, handling BLO duties, and participating in election-related activities. These duties significantly impact the time available for professional development activities and teaching.

#### 3. TRAINING ENGAGEMENT & FORMAT PREFERENCES

**In-person Training Experience & Value Drivers:** The teacher found in-person training sessions highly valuable due to group work and peer discussions, which allowed for immediate feedback and question resolution. These elements facilitated a better understanding of the subject matter and provided practical insights that could be directly applied in the classroom.

**Digital Training Perception:** The teacher perceived digital training (DIKSHA and iGOT) as less effective compared to in-person training. He highlighted the lack of real-time interaction, the difficulty in addressing doubts instantly, and the preference for direct communication and feedback during face-to-face sessions.

**Barriers to Digital Engagement:** Primary barriers included poor network connectivity and the lack of two-way interaction, which hindered the effective use of digital platforms for professional development.

#### 4. RETENTION & CLASSROOM TRANSFER GAPS

**Recall Analysis:** The teacher struggled to recall specific content from training modules and materials such as the Margdarshika booklet. While he appreciated the availability of such resources, he found it challenging to implement the learnings directly in the classroom without continuous reinforcement and practical application.

**Application of Learning:** The only classroom practice mentioned by the teacher that was linked to training was conducting group discussions. However, he was unable to provide specific examples or recall any other classroom activities influenced by the training, indicating a gap in the transfer of learning to the classroom.

#### 7. PEER LEARNING COMMUNITIES (CLSS / SHAIKSHIK SAMWAAD)

**Valued Aspects:** The teacher valued the peer learning communities for the opportunity to learn from other teachers’ experiences and share ideas. However, the context of being the only teacher in the school limited the practical application of these learnings within the immediate school environment.

**Facilitation Challenges:** There were instances where the facilitator's message was not communicated clearly, affecting engagement and understanding during sessions. This highlights the need for clearer communication and more structured facilitation to enhance the effectiveness of these communities.

**Single-Teacher Limitation:** Given that the school has only one teacher, the cascading of learnings from peer communities is limited. This underscores the need for alternative methods to support the teacher in applying peer insights in a single-teacher context.

#### 8. CLASSROOM OBSERVATION & MENTORING EXPECTATIONS

**Teacher's Mental Model of Observation:** The teacher envisioned classroom observation as a means to assess student performance, syllabus completion, and teaching practices. He emphasized the need for clear guidance on improving these areas.

**Expectations from CACs/Academic Mentors:** The teacher expected mentors to provide live classroom demonstrations and practical support to improve teaching methods. This suggests a preference for hands-on, in-classroom support over theoretical guidance.

#### 9. ACTIONABLE POLICY & SYSTEMIC RECOMMENDATIONS

**Tailored Professional Development Programs:** Given the single-teacher context, professional development programs should be tailored to provide direct, practical support. This includes in-person training sessions with hands-on workshops and demonstrations.

**Enhanced Digital Platforms:** Improvements in digital platforms should address connectivity issues and provide mechanisms for real-time interaction and doubt resolution. Additional features like offline access to training materials can also enhance the utility of these platforms.

**Support for Single-Teacher Schools:** Policies should recognize and address the unique challenges faced by single-teacher schools. This could involve allocating additional resources for administrative support or providing more focused professional development that caters to these schools.

**Peer Learning Facilitation:** Clear facilitation guidelines and structured content should be provided for peer learning communities to ensure that teachers from single-teacher schools can effectively engage and benefit from these platforms.

**Classroom Observation & Mentorship:** The model of classroom observation and mentorship should focus on practical, hands-on support, with mentors providing direct classroom demonstrations and immediate feedback. This approach would be more relevant and beneficial in the single-teacher context.

This report provides a detailed analysis grounded in the evidence from the CPD EDS, highlighting the need for comprehensive and context-specific professional development support for teachers in Gwalior district. The recommendations aim to enhance the effectiveness of professional development initiatives and support the continuous improvement of teaching practices.
